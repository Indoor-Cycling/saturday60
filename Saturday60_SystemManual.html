THE SATURDAY 60 — System Manual

The Song BPM Selector, the MOWL-Style Build Sheet, the Drops Library Editor, the Spin Class Pro workbook, and the Spotify → Chosic → MOWL pipeline · rev. 26 · September 2026
The three HTML tools now run as one installed web app at https://indoor-cycling.github.io/saturday60/

0 · THE PHILOSOPHY AND THE THREE RULES

Every tool is built around one idea: PLAYLIST FIRST — MUSIC LEADS. Song structure (drops, choruses, builds) dictates interval timing; the clock follows the music on work segments, and only the fixed pieces — warm-up, bridges, cool-down — hold clean clock time.

Beat-locked RPMLegs ride the beat one of exactly two ways: 1:1 (RPM = BPM) or ½ (RPM = BPM ÷ 2). Nothing else. On import, anything over 110 BPM is set to ½ automatically — unless a per-song override says otherwise.
110 RPM ceilingEvery interval is capped at 110. Songs may be faster; legs never are.
Rider zonesREV 24Power and HR targets derive from your FTP and maximum heart rate, set in the Build Sheet’s header line and stored on the device (Keiser M3 / M3i Studio Plus, 72 gears). The template ships with FTP 158 W and Max HR 164 bpm.

2 · THE SONG BPM SELECTOR

The gauge and the phrase strip

Type a BPM or nudge with the −5 / −1 / +1 / +5 steppers. Cards list every block that tempo fits with its beat-locked RPM; anything faster than the cap gets the half-time rescue note. Beneath the number, the phrase strip shows how long 1, 8, 16 and 32 bars run at that tempo — at 126 BPM a bar is 1.90 s and a 32-bar phrase is 61 s.

The tap-tester

Tap the TAP pad in time with the music (4+ taps; keep going to refine). A 2-second pause resets it. Use sends the measured BPM to the gauge and the playlist form. This is the referee whenever a database's BPM looks doubled or halved.

Playlist builder

Add title + BPM; songs file under their best block and move with the per-song block dropdown. Copy list exports everything grouped by block, drops and overrides included.

Removing a songREV 19The × takes two taps. The first turns it into a red Delete?; the second removes the song. It disarms itself after four seconds, if you tap anything else, or if you arm a different song's ×. Only one is ever armed at a time.
Hover helpREV 19Every button in all three tools carries a plain-language tooltip describing what it does. These appear on hover, so they help on a computer; a phone has nothing to hover with.

Per-song ride mode — the 1:1 / ½ button

Each song has its own ride-mode button that cycles three ways: follow the block (the default, unstyled), 1:1 forced, ½ forced. An overridden song lights up in the block's colour and its line reads "set". The override travels in transfer codes and beats the Build Sheet's automatic over-110 rule, so a deliberate choice is never silently undone.

Song lengthREV 19

On each song's drop-marker screen, under the downbeat field, is a song length box in m:ss, with a from timer button that takes whatever the stopwatch is showing. It is optional — leave it blank and nothing changes.

Why it existsRuntime used to live only on the Build Sheet, so importing a playlist could not fill it. A length set here travels with the song and lands in the sheet's Len column on import.
Where it showsOn the playlist row after the RPM — "126 BPM · 63 RPM · 3:42".
Where it travelsTransfer codes, backups, and Import ← Selector. Not stored in the drops library.
Bad inputAnything unreadable is refused and the previous value returns. Clearing the box removes the length.

Drop markers — the ◎ button

CapturingStopwatch (Start/Pause/Reset) plus a big Mark drop button; chips show what you caught, each removable. Add by hand takes typed times.
Five ways to captureREV 23Split-screen with Spotify and mark live · split-screen with Musixmatch and tap when the lyrics flip to Chorus (the drop sheet fits a half-height window) · read Spotify’s elapsed clock and type it (most accurate) · scout the YouTube most-replayed peak in advance · hold a phrase boundary in the grid and take the time straight off it.
Simultaneous startTap the song's restart control and the stopwatch's Start together on your own count — you are launching both from zero, not catching a moving song.
Phrase boundariesAn 8 / 16 / 32 toggle lists every boundary time in the song — the natural places a drill can start or end. On the 8-bar view every fourth marker is brightened because it is also a 32-bar boundary; any boundary near one of your drops is highlighted. The list runs eight phrases ahead of the downbeat and extends as you mark drops past it; switch to 32 bars to see further into the track at a glance.
Holding a boundaryREV 23Press and hold any time in the phrase grid for half a second and it becomes a captured drop — no stopwatch, no typing. A bar sweeps across the chip as you hold, the phone gives a short buzz when it takes, and the boundary turns green to show it is now one of your drops. Hold a green one and it comes back out again, so the same gesture is its own undo. A short tap does nothing and sliding your finger cancels, so scrolling the grid never captures by accident. This is the fastest route when you already know the structure and are choosing where a drill should start.
SnappingEach captured drop shows the boundary to snap to and the gap: "1:16 (−2.2s)", or "on the 8" in green when it lands square (drops are held in whole seconds, so anything inside that half-second of rounding counts as square). A drop sitting well off the grid is telling you that moment is not phrase-aligned — choose deliberately rather than assume.
First downbeat atThe grid assumes bar 1 starts at 0:00, which is wrong for tracks with an intro. Set this field (per song) and the whole grid shifts. It travels in the drops library and the transfer codes, so it is set once per song, not once per week.
Song-relativeDrops are stored as positions inside the song — durable, reusable, unaffected by playlist position or crossfade.
On a short screenREV 20The sheet never grows taller than the screen; if its content does not fit it scrolls inside itself. The title row and the × stay pinned at the top while you scroll, and a full-width Done button sits at the foot. This matters in split screen — at half height the sheet used to overflow upward and take the × off the screen with it.

Colour-coding the dropsREV 19

Tap a drop chip and it steps to the next colour in the key. Seven taps takes it all the way round and back to plain, so a mis-tap is undone by carrying on tapping. The chip shows the time as before, tinted — no words are added, and nothing about the colours reaches the Build Sheet or the Cue Notes.

The keySix colours sit above the chips with their names, shown once a song has drops. Defaults: Intro · Build · Drop · Chorus · Hook · Breakdown.
Edit keyRename any entry and recolour it from the phone's colour picker. Reset returns all six names and colours to the originals.
Restyling repaintsA drop stores a slot number, not a colour. Recolour slot 1 and every drop coded slot 1 changes with it, across every song.
Very dark coloursA near-black choice would vanish against the dark screen, so the editor names any entry at risk. It does not stop you.
Where it travelsBackups, transfer codes and the drops library. The Build Sheet and the Drops Library Editor carry the coding through untouched without displaying it.

Bands, defaults, transfer and backup

Bands & RPM settingsEvery block's BPM range and ride mode, plus the universal RPM cap. Save as my defaults sets your baseline; Reset to my defaults returns to it; Factory reset restores the originals.
Send to sheet / Import codeA transfer code carrying playlist, BPMs, blocks, per-song modes, drops, colour coding, song lengths, band settings and saved defaults. Paste a Build Sheet code in by mistake and it tells you which button to use instead.

Where a saved file goesREV 22There is no separate Save to… button any more — every Backup, Export file, Save file and Export CSV button asks where the file should go. On a phone that is the share sheet, so it can go to Drive, Files or mail; on a computer it is the ordinary save dialog, so it lands in a folder you pick. A browser that can do neither still downloads as before. See Section 7.
3 · THE BUILD SHEET

Structure

Segment numbers renumber themselves to match ride order. Renaming a sectionREV 26The title in a coloured header is a field. Click it and type; the songs in that section do not move. The working note underneath is editable the same way. Both print as plain text — no boxes, no outlines.
Switching two sectionsREV 26The ⇄ menu in a header trades that section's whole identity — title, note and colour — with another section's. Every song stays exactly where it sits. Getting the stock title backREV 26Once you rename or switch a section, this file stops refreshing its title, note and colour from the template — your wording is yours. A section you never touched keeps picking up template improvements as before.

FTP and Max HRREV 24

Getting drops into the sheet — which route

Three routes exist and they overlap. The choice depends on where the ride was built:

Sheet built from a CSVImport drops only. In the Selector tap Send to sheet, move the code across, then paste it into the Import drops box. It fills the Drop times column and nothing else — BPM, ride mode, position, intervals and targets are left exactly as they are.
Ride built on the phoneImport ← Selector. Same code, different button: it fills songs, BPMs, ride modes, drops and lengths into matching slots by block role.
Different devices, or for reuseDrops library. Export the .json from one tool and import it in the other. Slower, but it accumulates week to week and also carries the drill, the downbeat offset and the colour coding.
Both tools on one deviceSkip the transfer — the clipboard code goes straight from one to the other, and both tools now read the same storage.
Do not do bothImport drops only and Import ← Selector read the SAME code. Run one or the other, not both.

The drops library — moving it between the tools

Both tools read and write the same file, Saturday60-Drops-YYYY-MM-DD.json. Since the tools moved to one web address they also share one stored library on a given device, so the file is only needed to move between devices or to keep an archive. An entry holds the song, its drop times, its colour coding and its drill.

The Intervals sheet

One slot per cadenceOrder and repetition don't matter — only total seconds at each RPM. A Tabata of 8 × (20s on / 10s off) is two slots: 160s at the work cadence, 80s at the recovery cadence.

Keyed to row positionIntervals belong to a row, not a title. New week, new songs — clear them (ClearPlaylist does).

Cadence bands — not power zones

Time in Cadence Bands (Low under 70 · Mid 70–89 · High 90+) measures legs turning, not watts, and is named that way to avoid confusion with the true power zones on the Build Sheet. A light-geared song can sit in the High band while barely taxing you.

A watts-per-interval version with real Z1–Z5 and a planned TSS is possible but not built: it roughly doubles data entry, and normalized power computed from interval blocks overstates short efforts because it skips the 30-second smoothing real NP uses. Any such figure would be planned load, not what the bike measures.

6 · THE TIMING AND PHRASE MATH

Crossfade

With a 10 s crossfade, real clock time = sum of lengths −10 s per transition. A 16-song hour plays ~2:30 faster than its raw sum. All Σ figures use this, and MOWL was verified to use the same convention.

Song-relative vs ride-clock drops

Stored drop times are positions inside the song. The ◉ ride line converts them to class minutes: song start = Σ −length, drop = start + offset.

Fractional drift

Tracks carry fractional seconds that whole-second interval maps truncate, so music falls ~0.4–0.5 s further behind the intervals each transition — measured at 6.5 s over 15 transitions. The Drift s/song field (0.43 measured; 0 = show MOWL's clock) corrects ◉ times only; Σ stays matched to MOWL. Recalibrate: gap at the last song ÷ transitions. In MOWL, stretching every other interval by +1 s holds the hour within ~half a second.

Bars and phrase boundaries

Assuming 4/4: one beat = 60 ÷ BPM, one bar = 240 ÷ BPM. At 126 BPM a bar is 1.90 s, 8 bars 15.2 s, 32 bars 61.0 s. The tools work in boundary times rather than bar numbers, because what you need is where a drill can start, not where you happen to be.

8 is the working gridDance and pop is built in 8-bar sections, with 16 and 32 as the bigger groupings. Most drops land on an 8; 32-bar alignment is much rarer, since a 32-bar phrase runs about a minute and a 3½-minute song holds only three or four.
When a drop misses the 8Every drop off by the same amount → the downbeat offset is wrong, not the music (by far the most common cause). One drop off by a beat → you marked the impact, not the downbeat. Off by 2 or 4 bars from a point onward → the song has an odd-length section, common in remixes and sped-up edits. Scattered fractions → stopwatch noise; a waveform editor removes it.
What actually mattersIt matters less that the drop sits on a boundary than that your effort STARTS on one. Begin a sprint at the 8-bar mark before the drop and the drop lands inside the effort — riders get a bar or two to load up rather than reacting cold.

The phrase map — boundaries on the ride clock

The Build Sheet's Phrase map converts every song's boundaries into class time using the same crossfade and drift maths as the ◉ ride column, so a boundary can be typed straight into Session Designer. It needs only BPM and Len, so a ride is programmable from the CSV alone — drops become refinement rather than a prerequisite.

match MOWL (default)Ignores drift. These times line up with Session Designer.
real audioApplies your drift setting. These match what the room hears, and run later than MOWL's clock — at 1.0 s/song the third song is already 2 s adrift, which is how a mismatch usually announces itself.
Per-song offsetA small field on each row of the map, for tracks that open before bar 1.

This is the arithmetic half of what a DJ phrase counter does. The other half — finding the true first downbeat and confirming the tempo holds — needs the audio file, and is worth doing in a waveform tool for the songs where phrasing really matters; read the downbeat there and type it into the offset field.

Where data livesREV 19In the browser's storage on the device where you typed it, keyed to the web address. All three tools now share one address, so they share one storage area on that device — the Selector's drops library is the Build Sheet's drops library. Nothing is uploaded; there is no account and no server.
Per device, stillStorage does not follow you between phone and laptop. Backups and transfer codes are how work moves.
Data from the old loose filesREV 19A browser kept separate storage for every file path, so nothing saved in the standalone HTML files carries over. Open the old file, press Backup, then press Restore in the app. Do it for the Selector and the Build Sheet once.
Backup files (best)Each tool's Backup button writes a dated .json; Restore reads it back on any device at any time, and unlike clipboard codes it works AFTER the fact. The drops library exports the same way.
Saving to the cloud, and .txtREV 22On a phone the save buttons hand the file to the share sheet, because that is the only route to Drive, Files or mail — the phone’s own file picker sees local storage only, and Chrome on Android offers no download-folder choice on a handset with no SD card. On a computer the same buttons open a real save dialog instead.
Transfer codes (quick)Selector ↔ Selector, Sheet ↔ Sheet, and Sheet → Selector. Snapshot, not live sync — the device you Send from wins. The paste box accepts codes of any size and cleans line breaks from email wrapping.
What a full backup isA Selector backup + a sheet backup + the drops library: playlist, drops, colour coding, song lengths, bands, defaults, ride modes, the whole sheet, the Section Library, the drill catalog and the drift calibration.
When to back upMonthly, and always before uninstalling the app or clearing browsing data. Removing an installed app can clear its storage on some phones.
8 · TROUBLESHOOTING
Import says "No songs found"The CSV header wasn't recognised. The importer knows Chosic (Song/Artist/BPM/Duration) and Exportify (Track Name/Artist Name(s)/Duration (ms)).
"That code could not be read"Usually the tail was cut in transit, or it's the wrong code type. Re-copy with the whole block selected; compare the last 10 characters on both devices.
An Elapsed Time is blankRun RepairFormulas. See Section 5.
Three print pages instead of twoStale page breaks from an earlier run. Fixed — FormatPlaylistPrint now resets them every time.
A BPM looks doubled or halvedSpotify-derived tempo data does this. Tap-test and edit the BPM.
Times don't match a databaseDatabases (and AI memory) get durations wrong; the Chosic CSV is the referee.
Key/Camelot codesKey detection runs well under 50% accurate on some services. Match within segments only; bridges are free reset points.
Explicit versionsSpotify search and Spotlistr default to the ▪ version. Audit the Chosic Explicit column.
Smart ReorderPremium, mobile-only. Useful within a block; it will sand off the deliberate tempo cliffs between blocks.
Intervals drift from the musicSection 6. Growing drift = fractional seconds (set Drift); constant steps = crossfade mismatch; irregular = Automix snuck on.
Boundaries look wrongThe track opens with an intro — set the downbeat offset for that song.
Phrase map disagrees with MOWLThe map is on the real-audio clock. Switch it to match MOWL, and check the drift value is your measured one (0.43), not a guess.
Only nine phrase boundaries listedWorking as designed: the grid runs eight phrases past the downbeat and grows as you mark drops. Switch to 32 bars to see further ahead.
Lengths went blank after an importREV 19A slot that changed to a different song loses the old song's runtime, because it would otherwise corrupt the ride clock. Amber boxes mark what to fill. Set lengths in the Selector and they arrive with the songs instead.

9 · THE APP ONLINE — INSTALLING, UPDATING, ROLLING BACK

The three HTML tools are published as one installable web app at https://indoor-cycling.github.io/saturday60/. Opening that address gives a hub screen with the three tools on it. The whole app is stored on the device the first time you visit, so it runs with no signal at all — a basement studio or a phone in aeroplane mode makes no difference.

Installing · iPhone / iPadOpen the address in Safari (Chrome on iOS cannot do this). Share button → Add to Home Screen → Add. A "Sat 60" icon appears with your other apps.
Installing · Android / desktopThe hub's Install on this device button appears when the browser is ready. Otherwise: Chrome ⋮ → Add to Home screen, or the install icon at the end of a desktop address bar.
Do you have to install it?No. It works the same in an ordinary tab, offline included. Installing gives it its own window and icon, and browsers protect an installed app's storage more carefully. Both read the same data on that device.
Which version am I on?The hub shows it — "Version s60-v12" — with a Check for updates button beside it. Or open …/saturday60/sw.js and read the VERSION line near the top.

The files in the repository are the only source of truthREV 21

A copy in a downloads folder, attached to an old message, or pasted into a chat weeks ago is not. Before asking anyone — or any AI assistant — to revise a tool, fetch the current file from the repository and hand over that. Every tool carries shared machinery an older copy will silently lack: the app shell (app.js, the manifest and icons, which provide the back-to-hub bar, the save-destination prompts and the offline install), the drops colour coding, and the song length that travels into the Build Sheet. A revision built from an earlier copy removes those without saying so, and the loss shows up later as missing colours or a page that no longer works offline. The repository's README.md says this, and every file opens with a comment saying the same, so the warning travels with the file even when nobody thinks to mention it.

Publishing a change

The app is hosted from a GitHub repository. Editing it is uploading files.

1 · Replace the filesIn the repository: Add file → Upload files, drag in what changed, Commit changes. A matching filename overwrites; nothing duplicates. When in doubt, upload the whole folder — it costs one extra drag and removes any doubt about which version is live.
2 · Bump the versionOpen sw.js and raise the number on the VERSION line near the top: var VERSION = 's60-v13';. That one word is what tells installed copies there is something new. Forget it and the update still lands, just a launch or two later.
3 · Take it on the phoneWait about a minute for GitHub to republish, open the app, and tap Update now on the bar that slides up. Your songs, drops and sheets live on the phone and are never touched by an update.
If no bar appearsCheck the repository finished publishing (green tick), then open …/sw.js and confirm the VERSION line near the top shows the new number, then tap Check for updates, then close the app fully and reopen it.

Rolling back

A version number only ever goes forward. To undo a change you upload the older files under a higher version number — re-uploading an older number would leave every installed copy unaware anything had changed.

HowUpload the previous files with sw.js stamped higher than the current one, for example 's60-v13-rollback'. Commit, wait, tap Update now.
Your dataUntouched. A feature that stored something extra leaves that data dormant rather than deleting it, and rolling forward again brings it back.
Keep the zipsThe simplest safety net is keeping the last known-good folder somewhere. There is no version history to fall back on beyond what GitHub itself records.

Privacy

The repository is public, which is what free hosting requires — so the tools' code is readable by anyone with the address. Your content is not: songs, BPMs, drop times, colour coding and build sheets are written to storage on the device you typed them on and never leave it. The app has no server and asks for no account.
